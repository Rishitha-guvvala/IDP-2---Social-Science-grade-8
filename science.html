THE SCIENCE BEHIND DYECYCLE

How can used dyeing
water become usable again?

Understanding the science behind treating and reusing textile wastewater.

🔬 Understanding Textile Wastewater

Textile dyeing produces wastewater containing dyes, salts, fibres, organic substances and other chemical residues. Treating this water before reuse can reduce freshwater demand and wastewater discharge.

🧪 1. Coagulation

Coagulation is a treatment process in which chemicals help tiny suspended particles join together into larger groups called flocs.

One chemical that can be used is aluminium sulfate:

Al2(SO4)3

When it dissolves in water:

Al2(SO4)3 → 2Al3+ + 3SO42−

The Al3+ ions help destabilize suspended particles, allowing them to form larger flocs that can be separated from the water.

💧 2. Filtration

The formed flocs, fibres and other larger particles can then be removed through filtration.

🔬 3. Further Treatment

Additional methods, such as membrane filtration, can help remove smaller dissolved substances and remaining pollutants. The treatment required depends on the wastewater quality and the intended use of the recovered water.

♻️ 4. Water Reuse

After suitable treatment and quality testing, recovered water can be reused for appropriate textile processes instead of always taking fresh water.

MATHS — USING DATA TO MEASURE CHANGE

How can data help us
understand textile water use?

Using mathematical calculations to measure and compare water consumption.

📊 Understanding the Data

To understand whether a textile process can reduce water consumption, we used real research data comparing regular dyeing and cationic dyeing.

The data was entered into Excel and used to calculate:

💧 Water Saved

📉 Percentage Reduction

🧮 Our Calculations

Water Saved

Regular Dyeing − Cationic Dyeing

Percentage Reduction

(Water Saved ÷ Regular Dyeing) × 100

🧮 Example: First Black Sample

Water Saved:

89 − 57 = 32 L/kg

Percentage Reduction:

(32 ÷ 89) × 100 = 35.96%

This means that, in this sample, the cationic dyeing process used 35.96% less water than the regular dyeing process.

📈 What Our Data Shows

Across the six samples in the research data, cationic dyeing used less water than regular dyeing in every case.

The calculated reductions range from 20% to 36.47%.

This shows how mathematical analysis can help us measure and compare the environmental impact of different textile processes.

How did textile production become so resource-intensive?

As textile production expanded to meet growing demand, manufacturers developed processes that rely heavily on water and chemical inputs, particularly during bleaching, dyeing, printing and finishing. These “wet processing” stages are major environmental hotspots because they require repeated water use and generate wastewater containing chemical residues.

The growth of fast fashion and frequent production has added further pressure. The demand for new styles and large volumes of clothing encourages faster, high-volume production, making resource efficiency increasingly important.
